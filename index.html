Settings

Play

Chords

The seven buttons play the chords of the key: 1, 4 and 5 are major; 2, 3 and 6 are minor; 7 is diminished. They never sound bad together. Try 1 → 5 → 6 → 4.

Hold a button and move the joystick: each direction changes the colour of the chord. Let go and you are back to the normal chord. In Settings → Chords you can choose between five joystick maps.

The screen shows the name of the chord that is sounding; if you also want to see it on the keys, turn on "Notes and numbers on keys" in Settings → Look. That option applies to every mode: with it off, the keys stay clean in Drums, Beats, Mixer, Seq and the rest (only quick select always shows the names). In Settings → Chords, "Bass" adds the root an octave down (Root) or lets you hold one button for the bass and press another for the chord (Slash), and "V.Lead" (Voice Leading) chooses the inversions for you (the INV key is then dimmed).

Tools

They act on the last button you played, which you can recognise by its green light. INV changes the inversion. LOCK keeps that button on the last variation you played; press it again to release it. The two keys below, joined like a rocker, raise or lower only that button by an octave.

The light on each key shows its state: inversion on, chord locked, or octave shifted down or up. In Sequencer, Mixer, Notes Hero and Ear Trainer they are the same four keys, in the same place and with the same colours: only the icon and the text underneath change, and that text says what each one does; in Drum, Drum Loop, Mic Sample and Tuner they are not used and disappear.

Wheel

The wheel at the top right turns when you slide a finger left or right (on a computer, also with the mouse wheel or with the arrow keys when it is selected). On its own it sweeps the filter: to the right is brighter, to the left is darker. With the key on screen held it sets the attack; with the sound, the release; with the mode, the filter, as on the device. In the effects menu, on Reverb or Delay, it sets the amount. In Mixer, with a track held, it sets its volume, and turning it keeps that track from being muted. In Mic Sample it sets the microphone gain (Mic Gain). The small text next to the wheel says what it controls at any moment, and the screen shows the value while you turn it.

The overall volume is controlled by your device's volume buttons: a web page cannot read them, which is why the wheel is used for the filter and the rest.

Dimmed controls

A dimmed button or tool has no function in the current mode (for example, empty tracks in the Mixer) and does not respond to touch.

Screen and shortcuts

Settings on the screen

The gear on the screen opens the settings inside it; another tap, or Esc, closes them. The first line is the tab (Look, Music, Sound…): with it selected, left and right change tab. Up and down move through the rows; left and right change the value of the selected row; a tap on the knob (or Enter) activates it: it toggles a checkbox, runs a button, sets the tempo or loads and saves presets. The wheel moves the slider of the selected row. You can also tap the rows and the ‹ › arrows, and slide the list with your finger.

While the settings are open, the chord buttons keep sounding, so you hear every change. Reports (diagnostics, microphone test and vibration test) and the instrument code are read inside the screen itself: up and down (or the wheel) scroll the text, left goes back to the list and, for the code, "Copy" puts it on the clipboard. "Reset the app" asks for a second tap ("Sure?"). Importing a backup opens the system file picker. The result of an action (for example "Sound reset") appears for a moment in the title bar of the settings. Only this manual opens in the settings sheet.

Menus

Tap the key, the sound or the mode on the screen (a short tap, like the grey, yellow and red buttons of the device) and its menu opens inside the screen. With the joystick, left and right change the value, and up and down move to the submenus: on the key, up and down raise or lower the octave of everything; on the sound, up enters the list of effects and settings (left and right move through it, up changes the option, down goes back to the sound; on Reverb and Delay the wheel sets the amount); on the mode, up enters the BPM and down enters the mode setting (speed, kit, variation or level). Holding the joystick to one side repeats the step. You can also tap the neighbours on the left and right and the hints at the bottom of the screen.

The sound menu draws the sound itself: one note of it, played silently with your current settings (Attack, Release, brightness, pair detune), so you see its real shape, how it starts, holds and fades. When the shape seems to breathe or flatten for a moment, that is the detuned pair of oscillators drifting against each other, which is also what you hear; with Pair detune at 0 the shape keeps steady.

The menu closes when you play a chord, with a tap on the knob, by tapping the label again, or after a few seconds of doing nothing. In Notes Hero and Ear Trainer only the mode menu opens.

Quick select

Hold the sound, the key or the mode on the screen for a moment: the seven buttons turn into choices (the current one is marked dark). The first page is the same as on the device. Sound: 1 Saw, 2 Sine, 3 FM EPiano, 4 HX7 Piano, 5 Strings, 6 Clarinet, 7 FM Wow; in Drum and Drum Loop it chooses the kit (1 Tight Kit, 2 x0x Box, 3 x9x Box, 4 Lynn Kit, 5 KR-78, 6 Trap Box, 7 Tight Kit). Mode: 1 Play, 2 Strum, 3 Lead, 4 Drone, 5 Arp, 6 Repeat, 7 Mic Sample. Key: in Arp it chooses the pattern (1 Up, 2 Down, 3 Up/Down, 4 Down/Up, 5 Random, 6 Fingerpick) and 7 changes the layers (Arp Only, Chord+Arp, Rhythm+Arp); in the other modes, the notes and the scales.

With the joystick up or right you go to the next page, with the rest of the sounds or modes; down or left, to the previous one. On the keyboard: J (sound), K (key) and L (mode), held down. Sound and key do not respond in Notes Hero or Ear Trainer.

Randomise with the knob

Hold the key, the sound or the mode on the screen and, with your other finger, tap the joystick knob with a short tap, without moving it (like pushing the joystick down on the device). Key: changes everything, that is, the sound, the effects, the key and the tempo. Sound: a new sound (waveform and effects); the key, the tempo and the inversions stay. Mode: a new pattern (arpeggio, rhythm or sequence, and the octave layout); the sound stays. This is what the device does in its 3.0 version. It does not count if a chord is held, nor in Notes Hero and Ear Trainer.

On a computer, hold J (sound), K (key) or L (mode) and press the space bar. For more control, Settings → Presets has randomisation with more options (see "Presets and random").

Modes and rhythms

Modes

Play full chord. Strum strummed chord. Lead one note, the root. Drone the chord keeps sounding when you let go. Arp arpeggio at the tempo. Repeat the chord pulses at the tempo. To change mode, tap the mode on the screen (bottom left) and move with the joystick, or hold it for quick select (see "Menus" and "Quick select"); each mode's setting (speed, kit, variation or level) is in that same menu, downwards.

Drum each button is a hit; hold a pad and move the joystick to repeat it. Drum Loop each button starts a rhythm (and stops it). Sequencer adds chords to a sequence and plays it with play. Mixer mutes tracks and controls the metronome.

In Sequencer, the keys at the bottom are play and stop, remove the last step, clear and the step length. In Mixer, with a track selected, they are solo, vol −, vol + and clear.

Repeat: trance gate

In Repeat, hold a chord and it opens and closes to the beat of the tempo following a fixed 32-step pattern, with rests and tied notes, like a trance synth. The pulse speed is changed in the mode menu, downwards (the same speeds as the arpeggio). The release is limited to 200 ms so the cuts stay clean.

Fill

In Drum Loop, the Fill variation replaces the fourth beat of every bar with a snare and tom roll. Every four bars the fill takes the second half of the bar and ends with a cymbal on the next downbeat.

Sound

Instruments and library

The drop-down in Settings → Sound gathers three kinds of sound. Instruments are the base sounds (saw, piano, strings, organ…). Library are 50 presets from the official app, grouped by type (Analog, FM, Sample, Texture, Hybrid): each one combines several layers and brings its own envelope and effects, which are applied when you choose it. They are included and cannot be removed. Imported appears only if you load a .json file in Settings → Presets; there you can also see how many there are and remove them.

Presets and random

In Settings → Presets there are four slots (P1 to P4). Save writes the instrument, the effects, the key, the scale, the mode, the tempo, the octaves, the inversions and the locked chords into the slot; Load restores them. Loops, the theme and the volumes are not saved.

Random suggests new things. Sound chooses another instrument with effects; Pattern changes the pattern of the current mode (arpeggio, rhythm or sequence) and the octave layout; All also changes the key and the tempo.

With the key, the sound or the mode held, tapping the joystick knob randomises instantly (see "Randomise with the knob").

Vocoder

Makes the sound "talk" with your voice. In Settings → Effects, choose Mic (your voice) or Loop (what you recorded on looper track 1, which then stops sounding directly). Hold a chord and speak or sing: the synth takes the shape of your voice. It works best with rich sounds such as Saw or Square. Formant Shift changes the character of the voice (higher or lower) and Noise Level adds noise so that s, t and k are understandable. Use headphones: with the speaker it feeds back.

Record and capture

Looper

The circle arms the selected track; another tap starts recording. In free mode, a third tap stops. With bars, there is a four-beat count-in and it stops by itself. Tracks 2 to 6 wait for the start of the next loop to begin (their button blinks; tap it to cancel) and stop by themselves at the end of the pass.

Tap a track to select it; hold it to clear it. Clearing track 1 clears everything. ▶/⏸ pauses and resumes; hold it to clear everything. Recording while a Drum Loop rhythm is playing puts it on the track.

Save audio

The circle button, next to the wave, records what is sounding (chords, drums, loops and vocoder) for up to four minutes; the screen shows the time. Tapping it again stops it and opens the iPhone share menu, where you choose "Save to Files"; on a computer a 16-bit stereo WAV file is downloaded. If the app goes to the background during recording, it picks up where it was when you return, without a gap. It does not include the direct microphone.

Mic Sample (sampler)

Records a sound with the microphone and turns it into an instrument. Enter Mic Sample mode and grant permission. Hold button 1 while you sing, speak or make the sound; you can record up to three seconds. When you let go, the app detects the note, brings it to C and switches to Lead mode with the "Mic Sample" instrument: the buttons play your sound at different pitches. To record another one, enter Mic Sample mode again; the new take replaces the previous one, and the last one is kept when you close the app. In this mode only button 1 works; the rest are dimmed.

Tuner

Measures the note sounding near the microphone and tells you whether you are in tune. Enter Tuner mode, grant permission and sing or play a single long note near the iPhone. The screen shows three things: the name of the note (A, C#…); how many cents you are away from it (100 cents are a semitone; with + you are sharp and with − flat); and, below, a scale with a moving dot: to the right of the centre you are sharp and to the left flat. When you are within 5 cents, the screen inverts: you are in tune. If there is no sound or it is very weak, a dash appears. The thin line at the bottom shows how much the microphone hears: if it does not move when you sing, the sound is not arriving.

It measures notes between B1 and B5 (about 60 Hz to 1 kHz), one at a time: chords and background noise confuse it. If it does not detect your voice, move closer or raise "Mic Gain" in Settings → Sound. In this mode the buttons are dimmed because they are not used.

Microphone permission

The microphone is closed as soon as you finish using it, so Safari may ask for permission every time. To stop it asking, tap "aA" in the address bar → Website Settings → Microphone → Allow; the app will still close the microphone when it finishes. In Settings → Sound, "Test microphone" shows the state: "granted" means Safari already remembers it and "prompt" that it will ask again.

Alternative: turn on "Keep microphone open" in Settings → Sound. The microphone stays open but muted while the app is open (the iPhone's orange dot may stay on) and is released when you leave the app or after 15 minutes of not using it.

If the microphone does not work

If the notice says it is blocked, in Safari tap "aA" in the address bar, go to Website Settings and allow the microphone. If it says iOS could not start the audio, close Safari completely and open the page again; if it is still the same, restart the iPhone. "Test microphone", in Settings → Sound, shows the full state. If the app ever stops responding while using the microphone, open Diagnostics afterwards: it lists the last microphone steps, which tells where it stopped.

Practice

Notes Hero (chord game)

A rhythm game that teaches real songwriting moves. Choose a level in the mode menu, downwards (10 levels in five tiers: Folk, Rock, Modern, Jazz and Fusion); the level, the tier and the song title appear at the top of the screen. Press "play". The screen shows a single lane: at the top left, a circle with the button you need (and next to it an arrow if you must move the joystick), at the top right the key, at the bottom left the name of the chord and at the bottom right how many you have done. After a four-beat count-in, one ball per chord travels from left to right towards the hit line (with the hit zone shaded): press its button just as the ball reaches the line and, if it has an arrow above it, also hold that joystick direction. The thin stroke between two balls is how long that chord lasts. The ball fills in when you hit and is crossed out when you miss. The closer to the beat, the better the grade: Perfect, Great, OK or Miss. Your streak and best streak are counted. Each song sets its own key, sound and rhythm; your settings come back when it ends.

"Practice" plays slower: it starts at 75 % and with "speed −" and "speed +" goes from 50 to 100 %. After the results, "next" goes to the next level and "replay" plays the current one again. In the results, "mean offset" tells you whether you tend to press late (+) or early (−): if it is always similar, add it in Settings → Modes → "Game latency" (sound takes a moment to reach your ears and you can feel it); the ball should reach the line when you hear the hit. Each level introduces an idea: the chord 6 (level 2), sus4 with the joystick down (3), changes every two beats and anticipations (4), the borrowed iv with the joystick up (5), the mix with 2 and 3 (6), sevenths (7), ninths (8), secondary dominants (9) and a chain of fifths (10).

To leave Notes Hero (or Ear Trainer), with the game stopped or finished, tap the mode name at the bottom left of the screen: it opens the mode menu and left and right take you to another one. During play that name disappears (only the status shows) and does not respond to touch, so you do not leave by accident: to cut it short, use "stop".

Ear Trainer

The app plays something and you repeat it. Choose a level in the mode menu, downwards, and press "play": first the base note (the tonic) sounds and then the question. Level 1: you hear the tonic and a higher note; press the button for that note. Level 2: the note can be above or below. Level 3: a chord of the key. Level 4: a progression of four chords. Level 5: a chord with a modification (sevenths, sus, aug…): answer with the joystick too. Level 6: a progression of four chords with modifications.

If you get stuck, "tonic" plays the base note again and "replay" repeats the question. Your streak and best streak are counted. On levels with modifications, a miss shows an arrow with the correct joystick direction. While you play, your settings are replaced by the trainer's and come back when it ends.

Settings and maintenance

Look

notes is a single instrument built in six editions, which you choose in Settings → Look → Case: CARBON (black), LUNAR (ivory with black controls and orange touches), VERTEX (90s console-pad grey, with coloured buttons), POCKET (beige with an olive screen and aubergine buttons, like an 80s handheld), AMBER (warm charcoal with cream and mustard keys, like a boutique synth) and GHOST (smoked plastic that shows the circuit board). The screen is independent and represents another technology: VECTOR (warm, high-definition OLED), PIXEL (the small 1-bit OLED of the HiChord: the whole screen is a grid of about 120 × 63 real pixels with a bitmap font, inverted title bars, framed selectors with arrows at the sides, an icon for every mode and the animated waveform of each sound), MOSS (green reflective LCD, like the first Game Boy: the grid only hints inside the dark parts and the frame casts its shadow at the top and left) and PHOSPHOR (analogue oscilloscope: black screen where the beam draws everything in green; the graticule is engraved in the glass with its centre cross and the signal is drawn relative to that cross, with the sweep triggered so the wave stays still and an automatic volts/div). On every screen, the sound selector draws the waveform of the sound, animated: it appears, is held and fades out the way that sound does. "By case" uses each edition's own, and you can combine them however you like. "Automatic" picks CARBON or LUNAR depending on the device's dark or light mode. The line that crosses the screen is the wave: it runs from one edge to the other; at rest it is flat; with "Wave" on it shows the signal that is sounding. In PHOSPHOR that line does not exist: a simulated oscilloscope replaces it (an electron beam that moves, with the glow the phosphor leaves as it fades), always on. In "Time (Y-T)" the sweep triggers on a rising edge so the wave stays still, with automatic volts/div and time/div that you can read at the bottom; in "XY (figures)" the signal is drawn against itself shifted by 90° and Lissajous figures appear (Settings → Modes → "Oscilloscope", only available with the PHOSPHOR screen). The chord keys are clean, without notes or numbers; turn them on with "Notes and numbers on keys". "Test vibration" checks whether your device can vibrate from the app and, if not, why: on the iPhone you need iOS 18 or later and System Haptics turned on (iPhone Settings → Sounds & Haptics).

MIDI

PC only (Chrome or Edge) and Android; Safari on iPhone does not support it, which is why the setting does not appear there. Turn it on in Settings → Sound. The white keys of any octave play buttons 1 to 7 (C is 1). The chord that sounds goes out as MIDI notes on every connected output (if an output sends the notes back to the input, the app ignores that echo). In Arp and Repeat the held chord is sent, not every hit.

Backup

Export saves your settings, presets, imported sounds and the last microphone sample to a file. Import restores that backup and also accepts the preset files of the official app.

Offline

Open the page once with internet and add it to the home screen (Share → Add to Home Screen). From then on it works offline. New versions are downloaded when you open it with internet.

Audio and lock screen

After 20 seconds of nothing being touched or sounding, the app suspends audio so it does not show as "playing"; the first tap wakes it up and the first note may take a moment. When you leave the app or lock the iPhone everything stops, including the loops.

If something fails

If nothing sounds when you press a button, a notice appears with the reason. Settings → Presets → Diagnostics runs a test (audio, a chord, microphone, MIDI, saved state, cache and the latest errors) and shows a report that you can copy or send to ask for help; the "audio parameters" line tells you whether a browser extension (privacy or anti-tracking) is blocking the audio: the app works around it by itself.

If something is stuck in a strange state, "Reset the app", in the same place, erases the settings, the presets and the cache and starts over; it asks you to confirm with a second tap. Before that you can save a backup with Export (see "Backup").
